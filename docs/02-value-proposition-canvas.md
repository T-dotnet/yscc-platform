## 1. Purpose

VPC means **Value Proposition Canvas**. This document separates the jobs people need to accomplish from the product capabilities intended to help them. It does not claim validated product–market fit, willingness to pay, cost savings, or improved clinical outcomes.

The supplied CMDCS persona draft (CP1) adds explicit pain/gain hypotheses and commissioning, governance, implementation, integration, and research stakeholders. These canvases adopt its profiles alongside the captured collection requirements; none establishes validated procurement requirements or product–market fit.

## 2. Canvas A — Person participating in assessment

Related persona: P-01 — Kai in [personas](03-personas.md), CP1 p. 5. The source adds progress visibility, shared decision-making, avoiding repeated storytelling, and cultural safety. A participant progress view is candidate CR-02; its MVP dashboard / Stage 2 self-report statement conflicts with S1 and needs D-21. Cultural safety requires authentic co-design, not assumptions about Kai's identity.

### Customer profile

| Dimension | Working profile |
| --- | --- |
| Functional jobs | Understand and complete the required intake with appropriate support; know what is pending and who acts next; understand an assigned request; provide personal answers through a suitable eligible mode; know whether answers were received; understand the next care step. |
| Emotional jobs — hypotheses | Feel informed, respected, and able to ask for help; avoid feeling tested or blamed for a pause, difficulty, or permitted nonresponse. |
| Social jobs — hypotheses | Participate with or without support as appropriate without having others incorrectly speak on their behalf. |
| Pains — hypotheses | Unclear purpose or answer visibility; lengthy or repeated questions; unsuitable shared-device/contact context; uncertain save state; expired link; distress or access barriers. |
| Required gains | Clear instructions, actual submission confirmation, support route, and no access to unrelated records. |
| Desired gains — hypotheses | Less avoidable repetition, manageable steps, suitable assistance, and confidence that their own perspective stays identifiable. |

### Value map

| Product/service | Pain reliever to test | Gain creator to test | Evidence / requirement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Eligible SMS or clinic tablet collection | Removes an unnecessary account step; offers an alternative when a contact/device context is unsuitable. | Participation in an approved context suited to the person. | Reported channels S1; FR-16, FR-18, FR-20. |
| Purpose/visibility introduction | Explains what is being requested and who may see it before answers are collected. | Informed participation and appropriate help-seeking. | Provisional policy S2; FR-22–FR-27. |
| Clear progress and save/submission states | Reduces uncertainty and repeated attempts when connectivity fails. | Confidence about what happened and what to do next. | Proposed; FR-08, FR-44–FR-45. |
| Item-specific nonresponse and support | Offers only clinically approved options; supports stopping or seeking help. | Participation without fabricated answers or pressure to claim completion. | CB1 informs distinction; FR-11, FR-26. |
| Respondent/recorder provenance | Prevents assisted or transcribed answers being silently labelled clinician-rated. | A recognisable person contribution. | S1, CB5; FR-17, FR-21. |

**Value hypothesis:** If requests are understandable, appropriately scoped, and reliable to complete, people will experience fewer avoidable barriers to contributing their own information. Participation rate alone is insufficient evidence: check comprehension, appropriate choice, and support needs as well.

## 6. Fit hypotheses and validation

### Current prototype evidence, not validation

The local prototype makes several hypotheses observable: Kai-style participant completion can use a clear introduction, help/exit, conditional sample questions, review and a neutral end state; Jess-style work can begin from an owned queue and continue through collection, review, follow-up and a report; and Tom/Ananya-style work can inspect a correction with provenance and history. Intake and referral queues also make ownership and waiting states visible. These are implemented sample interactions, not evidence that the personas, needs, gains, usability or operational impact have been validated with users.

The prototype uses fictional records, browser-local state and sample policies. It has no live participant contact, external referral, authenticated role enforcement, real instrument/scoring or governed reporting/release workflow. Test the hypotheses with representative people and approved content before treating an observed demo path as product fit.

| ID | Hypothesis | Test | Evidence of fit / warning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| V-01 | Participants understand purpose, visibility, and next step. | Teach-back after the introduction and confirmation screens. | Accurate explanation without coaching; submission alone does not establish comprehension. |
| V-02 | Eligible channel choice reduces avoidable barriers. | Compare observed task completion and assistance across mobile, tablet, and staff entry. | Fewer recoverable barriers without weakening identity/context checks; avoid unadjusted channel rankings. |
| V-03 | Staff distinguish task submission from full assessment completion. | Partial assessment and expired-link scenarios. | Correct next action and status interpretation; confusing “sent” with “done” invalidates fit. |
| V-04 | Provenance and history make comparisons more trustworthy. | Version-change, joint-completion, and imported-gap scenarios. | Staff identify unsupported comparisons and the actual source of answers. |
| V-05 | Operational workflows support accountable correction. | Verified-source and no-source correction tasks. | Correct actor path and complete audit without reopening collection. |
| V-06 | Family participation can be clear without over-sharing. | Separate supporter and guardian tasks. | Participants understand their role and cannot infer unrestricted access. |

Use [UX strategy metrics](06-ux-strategy.md) alongside qualitative evidence. No numerical target or baseline is yet established. A strong value claim requires both reduced friction and correct, informed, appropriately scoped behaviour.

## 7. Prioritisation and non-promises

First establish safe participation and record integrity, then efficient assessment/handover, then longitudinal operations. Advanced dashboards cannot compensate for ambiguous source data or permissions.

Do not promise shorter treatment duration, better clinical outcomes, automatic diagnosis, private individual devices, guaranteed SMS receipt, universal self-report eligibility, automatic family visibility, or real-time clinical monitoring. Each would require evidence or an explicitly approved capability beyond this canvas.
