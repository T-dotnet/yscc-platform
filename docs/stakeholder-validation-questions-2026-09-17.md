# YSCC stakeholder discussion questions

Version 0.1 · 17 September 2026 · Early direction-setting discussion

## Why we are asking

We are shaping a service that helps care teams collect information, understand changes over time and decide what to do next. This discussion is about the work and decisions the service should support. It is not a review of screens or navigation.

## Assumptions behind the current direction

The current direction assumes that support is a journey rather than a single visit. A care episode (or care period) would run from the person’s start of support to its planned end, so related assessments and follow-up stay together. Events would be dated milestones or changes during that period - for example a referral, medication change, service contact or significant change in circumstances - recorded separately from questionnaire answers. The report would bring the answers, events and follow-up into one review view. This is a starting hypothesis, not a settled model. We need stakeholders to confirm whether these concepts are needed, whether the boundaries make sense, and what should be combined or removed.

## Key terms

| Term | Working definition |
| --- | --- |
| **Care episode** | The period in which a person receives support from a service. |
| **Assessment** | A structured review within that period, which may include several modules and decisions. |
| **Collection point** | A planned baseline or follow-up occasion when information is requested. |
| **Questionnaire** | An approved set of questions and response options. |
| **Response** | Answers submitted for a questionnaire, with their source, dates and review state. |
| **Event** | Something relevant that happened during the care period, such as a change in medication, service or circumstances. |
| **Report** | A summary of information collected over time to support a care conversation. |
| **History** | A record of activity and changes, including who acted, when and what changed. |

## Care episode

1. What should define the beginning and end of a person’s period of care?
2. What information must care teams always know about that period, such as dates, owner, status or next step?
3. How should a new period of care be handled when someone returns, transfers or is referred again?

## Events

1. Which changes or circumstances are important enough to record during a person’s care?
2. What should each event include: date, source, details, impact, confidence or something else?
3. Who should be able to record, correct and see events, and should people or supporters see any of them?

## Report

1. What should appear first: the latest response, change over time, unanswered work, written comments or next actions?
2. How should repeated questionnaire answers be compared: Likert trends, response-by-response detail, a summary, or another view?
3. How should changes in written answers be shown so the question, previous answer, new answer and dates are clear?
4. What context must stay with every answer: questionnaire/version, respondent, date, source, care episode or review state?
5. What action should be possible after reading the report, such as recording a review, planning follow-up, adding an event or requesting a correction?
6. Which information belongs in the report, and which should remain in the assessment or history record?

## Priorities and safeguards

1. What must be included in the first release, and what can wait?
2. What information is too sensitive to show broadly, and what permissions or approvals are needed?
3. What evidence would give you confidence that the service is understandable, safe and useful?

These questions are for early alignment. They do not establish clinical policy, access rules or final terminology.
