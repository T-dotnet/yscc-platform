## Value starts at the point of care.

A simpler contribution process is valuable only when it remains understandable, appropriate and trustworthy.

### Kai / Young person

**Job:** Share how I am doing and understand the next step.

**Pain:** Repetition, unclear visibility and uncertainty about receipt.

**Response:** Eligible self-report, scoped consent choices, plain explanations, support and confirmed submission.

**Gain:** A clearer, more manageable contribution to care.

### Deb / family carer

**Job:** Provide useful context and understand how to help.

**Pain:** Feeling excluded or overloaded; unclear role boundaries.

**Response:** A separate appropriate task, clear visibility and distinct authority checks.

**Gain:** Involvement without assuming access to another person's answers.

### Jess / treating clinician

**Job:** Collect, interpret and act on the right information.

**Pain:** Duplicate entry, fragmented context and ambiguous status.

**Response:** An episode-centred plan, eligible channels, policy-dependent review and a traceable longitudinal record.

**Gain:** Less avoidable coordination effort and clearer clinical context.

### Scope distinction

The staff longitudinal timeline is current prototype evidence. Kai's personal dashboard and Jess's full governed ongoing-care record remain candidate capabilities with separate access and phase decisions.

### TEST THE VALUE

Can people explain purpose and visibility, complete an appropriate task, and understand what happened? Can staff identify what remains? Completion rate alone is not proof of fit.

## Turn reliable data into useful action.

Different evidence users need different products. A single dashboard is not the value proposition for everyone.

### Rachel, Tom and Ananya / Service improvement team

**Job:** Run the service and maintain reliable, comparable records.

**Pain:** Manual follow-up, unclear definitions and fragmented reporting.

**Response:** Owned quality work, verified-source correction and, if adopted, governed metrics and releases.

**Gain:** More interpretable operations and accountable resolution.

### Sam / implementation scientist

**Job:** Understand fidelity and help centres improve.

**Pain:** Data without local context; findings that do not reach practice.

**Response:** Candidate implementation views linked to centre context and improvement actions.

**Gain:** Evidence that leads to practical support and follow-up.

### Priya, David, Maya and Helen / System evidence stakeholders

**Job:** Commission, oversee, exchange or research for approved purposes.

**Pain:** Inconsistent data, weak comparisons and unclear access processes.

**Response:** Candidate governed reports, exchange contracts and study-specific request/release pathways.

**Gain:** Defensible interpretation and controlled use, not unrestricted care access.

### Evidence discipline

These pains and gains are hypotheses. No verified savings, clinical impact, missing-data baseline or research-turnaround improvement has been established.

### INVESTMENT QUESTION

Which user decision will each data product improve, and how will we test that? Require an owner, a defined output and an approval boundary before adding scope.
